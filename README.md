## 💡 Why I Built Vaidann

Running a family business involves much more than creating and selling products.

There are orders to record, payments to reconcile, discounts to manage, customers who return and monthly figures to review.

I wanted to make those processes easier to manage without introducing complicated software.

The idea was simple:

**What if we could manage the essential operations of our family business through one straightforward application?**

That question became Vaidann.

---

## ⚙️ What Vaidann Does

| Feature | Functionality |
| --- | --- |
| 📦 Order Management | Create and track orders with product sizes, quantities and automatically calculated amounts. |
| Customer Records | Maintain customer information, order history and repeat customer counts. |
| Payment Tracking | Record UPI and cash payments against orders. |
| Discounts | Apply order-level discounts and calculate adjusted totals. |
| Bill Records | Capture bill amounts and upload supporting bill images. |
| Staff Access | Manage staff sign-in and access using Firebase Authentication. |
| Monthly Statements | Review monthly business activity through generated statements. |
| CSV Export | Export order, payment and customer records for reporting and reconciliation. |

---

## 🔄 How It Works

**1. Create an order**

Enter the customer's details, select the product size and quantity, and review the calculated order amount.

**2. Manage discounts and payments**

Apply a discount where required and record payments made through cash or UPI.

**3. Maintain customer history**

Access previous orders and identify returning customers without recreating their records.

**4. Review business activity**

Generate monthly statements and export relevant records for reconciliation and further analysis.

The aim is to make everyday operations easier for the people actually managing the business.

### Development Approach

I approached Vaidann as a practical product build:

1. Identified the everyday operational requirements.
2. Mapped the order, customer and payment workflows.
3. Built the application around those workflows.
4. Connected authentication and database functionality.
5. Tested practical scenarios, including discounts, duplicate payment recording and connectivity issues.
6. Refined the application based on testing.

## ✨ What This Project Taught Me

Vaidann reinforced something I find particularly interesting about technology:

**The most valuable solution isn't always the most complex one. It's the one that makes someone's work easier.**

Building this application required me to think beyond individual features.

I had to consider how information moves between orders, customers and payments, how different people use the system and what happens when something goes wrong.

It brought together product thinking, workflow design, problem-solving and hands-on technical development.
